## About Section

I build durable websites for Kansas City small businesses and open-source tools for communities that can't afford enterprise software.

A website built with plain HTML and CSS is a file. It lives on a server. It doesn't need a platform to exist, and it won't break when some company decides to change its terms or raise its prices. What most small businesses are paying for with Wix or Squarespace is software they're renting, not a website they own. I build the version you own. $399 to build, $29 a month to keep running.

The other half of what I do is longer-horizon. Commonweave is a framework for community-owned infrastructure - shared directories, scheduling tools, resource registries - the kind of boring necessities that should just exist for any neighborhood that wants them, without a SaaS subscription attached. NeighborhoodOS is a civic AI platform built on top of that. A way for a neighborhood to have its own locally-run assistant that knows the context: who to call, what's available, what's actually happening on the ground. WormHole is the local AI agent harness I built on Windows, open-sourced on GitHub, that makes this kind of local-first AI infrastructure actually runnable without cloud dependency.

I'm most interested in building things that last and that real people own. If you're working on neighborhood infrastructure, community-owned software, or civic tech that's genuinely for the people it's supposed to serve, I'd like to know what you're building.
