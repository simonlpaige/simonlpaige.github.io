DESIGNED AROUND YOUR SPACE

Lighting, infrastructure and access belong in one conversation.

A theatrical lighting installation starts with the activities on stage. Presentations, music, worship and camera production each need different coverage, control and flexibility. The right plan considers the stage geometry and operator workflow together with fixture positions, power, data and maintenance access.

Overhead equipment also brings building and rigging questions into the project. During planning, identify existing hanging positions, anticipated equipment loads, approved attachment points and the parties responsible for structural review and rigging work. These responsibilities need to be defined with the owner and construction team before an installation scope is finalized.

PLAN THE SCOPE

From the first conversation to the handoff.

Stage and camera coverage

Discuss the performers, presenters, scenic elements and camera views. Plan fixture positions and control needs around the uses of your stage and your production team.

Power, data and controls

Coordinate lighting power, control networks, consoles and programming with the venue infrastructure. Consider how operators prepare a show and how staff return the room to its everyday setup.

Rigging coordination

Review access and overhead mounting requirements with the project team. Establish who verifies structures and loads, supplies rigging equipment, installs attachments and provides required inspections.

BEFORE YOU START

Questions to bring to the table.

What information helps with stage rigging planning?

Share available structural drawings, existing rigging documentation, overhead equipment lists and access constraints. The consultation should identify the responsible structural and rigging specialists and the reviews needed for your venue.

Can existing theatrical fixtures and consoles be retained?

We can evaluate that option during planning. Fixture condition, control compatibility, available power, mounting requirements and the production goals determine whether retaining equipment makes sense.
