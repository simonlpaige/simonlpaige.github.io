AVDOXA voice & style guide · team draft

Technical confidence.
Human clarity.

Use AVDOXA’s voice to make complex systems easier to trust across every market, while preserving the relationship-centered language and real project experience that built the company.

Proposed strategic position A relationship-driven AVL design, integration, training, and support partner for houses of worship, performance spaces, institutions, workplaces, and project teams.
01

Voice principles

Ready, not flashy

Lead with the room’s job, reliability, and operational confidence. Let the experience be the payoff-not an empty adjective.

Technical, not opaque

Use exact terms when they help a buyer decide. Translate jargon into implications for scope, schedule, security, or uptime.

Accountable, not heroic

Show how AVDOXA plans, coordinates, documents, and supports. Avoid savior language and unsupported superlatives.

Collaborative, not vague

Name the people in the room: owners, consultants, construction managers, architects, operators, IT, production teams, and procurement.

MoreClearPreparedSpecificSteady
LessHypedImmersive-everythingGenericSalesy

Write this

“We coordinate infrastructure, networks, acoustics, lighting, control, and closeout before those details become field conflicts.”

“A system-health review gives your team a practical list of risks, priorities, and next steps.”

Avoid this

“We deliver cutting-edge, immersive solutions that elevate every experience.”

“Our world-class experts provide best-in-class service for all your technology needs.”

03

Visual system

The visual language borrows from signal-flow diagrams, rack elevations, status indicators, and theatrical blackouts. The “signal path” is functional: it shows how AVDOXA carries responsibility from one phase to the next.

Primary full-color logo

Purple is the primary brand color. Blue carries technical confidence and information. Green signals readiness, progress, and action. Use the white logo on dark photography and the full-color logo on quiet white fields.

Photography

  • Use AVDOXA’s real rooms, technicians, racks, coordination details, and operational moments.
  • Favor evidence over spectacle: a labeled rack, commissioning session, or control position can carry more trust than an empty wide shot.
  • Keep people in context. Show who operates, builds, coordinates, or experiences the system.
  • Avoid generic stock photos of handshakes, glowing abstract networks, or equipment without a project story.

Manufacturer marks

  • Authorized marks may support technical credibility, but AVDOXA’s system thinking and accountability remain the lead story.
  • Use supplied logo artwork unchanged, at a readable size, with generous clear space on a quiet white or neutral field.
  • Describe the collection as a “technology ecosystem” or “representative manufacturers.” Use partner, dealer, or certification language only when that exact relationship is current.
  • Never imply every brand or product line is specified on every project. Selection follows the room’s requirements.
  • AVDOXA may accurately identify itself as an Alcons Audio distributor. Keep that relationship label attached to Alcons rather than applying it to the entire logo set.

Facts to verify before launch

  • Current SDVOSB certification status, UEI, NAICS, PSC codes, and procurement contact.
  • Exact managed-service response commitments, monitoring coverage, reporting cadence, and price structure.
  • Authorized client names, testimonials, outcomes, project partners, and measurable results.
  • Supported geography and whether “Kansas City” should be paired with regional or national reach.
  • GA4 conversions, CRM attribution, phone tracking, form routing, and service-request ownership.
